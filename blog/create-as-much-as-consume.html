02 the killer hooks era

your content dies in the first 2 seconds.

▶
give me 15 mins, and i'll make your hooks impossible to skip
kallaway · 634k views

kallaway breaks down something most people get wrong: they think a hook is just a catchy first line. nah. a hook is a curiosity loop — a gap between what the viewer knows and what they desperately want to know. and that gap has to itch.

the hook autopsy 🔪

①
delay. you take too long to get to the point. if the first 2 seconds don't communicate what the video is about, people are gone. nobody's waiting around for your "context."
②
confusion. your hook is trying to be clever but the viewer has no idea what they're watching. clarity beats creativity. every. single. time.
③
irrelevance. you hooked them but the hook has nothing to do with the actual content. clickbait works once. then your retention tanks forever.
④
disinterest. the topic just... doesn't matter to your audience. you can have the perfect hook formula but if nobody cares about the subject? doesn't matter.

the formula that actually works: context lean-in → scroll stop → contrarian snapback.

think of it like you're on a highway going 70mph. the context lean-in is the billboard that makes you look. the scroll stop is the weird detail that makes you slow down. the contrarian snapback is the unexpected turn that makes you pull over completely.

but here's what hit me hardest: one hook isn't enough. you need to keep opening new curiosity loops throughout the video. every 15-30 seconds, a mini-hook that makes the viewer think "wait what?" — otherwise they're scrolling to the next thing.

✦ nurslay's takeaway

stop writing hooks. start writing curiosity loops. a hook catches attention. a curiosity loop keeps it. the best creators don't hook once — they rehook every few seconds. that's why you can't stop watching their stuff.

03 the storytelling era

facts inform.
stories transform.

▶
give me 9min, and i'll improve your storytelling skills by 176%
philipp humm · 2.8m views

philipp humm's whole thing is stupidly simple but it hit different: zoom into the moment. don't summarize events. don't give a timeline. put the viewer inside the scene. make them feel like they're there.

most people tell stories like news anchors — "and then this happened, and then that happened." that's not storytelling. that's a recap. nobody cares about recaps.

5 elements of a story that sticks ✦

①
location. just name where you are. "i was sitting in the coffee shop" — the moment you say it, the viewer's brain builds the whole scene. you don't need to describe the wallpaper.
②
actions. what are you doing in that moment? "i opened my laptop and stared at the blank screen." skip the backstory. just drop us into the action.
③
inner dialogue. what are you thinking? "i thought — this is it. either i post this or i delete the app." this is what makes stories relatable. people hear their own voice in yours.
④
emotions. don't say "i was scared." show it. "my hands were shaking. i hit publish and immediately closed my phone." show emotions through actions, not adjectives.
⑤
dialogue. real conversations. "my friend said, 'just post it, no one's watching anyway.'" dialogue makes stories feel alive. not scripted.

"the goal of a good story is to zoom the listener into the moment and put them directly into the action."

— philipp humm

the avg great story is only 87 seconds long. you don't need a 10-minute monologue. you need 90 seconds of raw, vivid, zoomed-in storytelling that makes someone feel something real. that's it.

04 the strategy era

social media isn't hard.
it's misunderstood.

▶

▶
my actual social media strategy for 2026
alex hormozi · 735k views

kallaway dropped 5 uncommon truths. combined with hormozi's actual 2026 strategy, here's what nobody tells you:

the uncomfortable truths ★

①
social media is not social. it's media first, social second. you're not "connecting with friends." you're competing for attention in the most brutal marketplace ever built. treat it like media production. not a diary.
②
virality is a trap. one viral post doesn't build a business. consistency does. 50 posts that each get 10k views > 1 post that gets 500k. the compound effect of showing up is what actually wins.
③
fish where the fish are. stop trying to make every platform work. find where YOUR audience lives and go all in. hormozi tests ideas on twitter first, then expands to video once he knows what resonates.
④
build ecosystems, not islands. short form feeds long form. long form feeds newsletter. newsletter feeds product. every piece of content should connect to the next. isolated content dies fast.
⑤
value doesn't accrue at the media layer. views don't pay bills. building something behind the content — a product, a community, a service — is what turns content into a career. media is the funnel, not the business.

hormozi's SPCL framework — status, power, credibility, likeness — is how he decides what to post. every piece of content should do at least one: raise your perceived status, demonstrate your expertise, build credibility through proof, or make people like you more.

simple. but how many of your posts actually do any of these? exactly. ✦

05 the system era

consume with intent.
create with speed.

ok so here's the actual system i'm building for myself. this whole article is the proof of concept — i consumed 4 videos and turned them into this. here's how you do the same:

the 1:1 creation system ⚡

①
consume with a pen, not a pillow. when you watch something, don't just vibe with it. take notes. capture the key insight in one sentence. if you can't summarize it — you didn't understand it.
②
the 24-hour rule. if you don't create something from what you consumed within 24 hours, it's gone. your brain forgets. the inspiration fades. speed is the hack nobody talks about.
③
add your lens. nobody wants another summary. the internet has enough of those. what's YOUR take? your experience? your weird angle? that's what makes content yours. that's what makes it hit.
④
format for the platform. a twitter thread ≠ a youtube video ≠ a blog post. same ideas, different packaging. match the format to where your people are.
⑤
publish before it's perfect. the best content is content that exists. done > perfect. your 8th post will be better than your 1st. your 80th will be better than your 8th. but only if you hit publish.
watch → write read → thread listen → post save for later add to notion screenshot & forget

first three = creation. last three = organized consumption pretending to be productivity. you know which camp you've been in. i know which camp i've been in. time to switch. ★
